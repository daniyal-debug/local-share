How it works

  1. 1

    Your browser gets a plate

    Something like KRT-482, kept for this browser and remembered across restarts. Letters that look alike — I, L, O and U — are never used, so a plate is safe to read aloud.

  2. 2

    Type someone else's plate

    The name of the device appears as soon as the plate resolves. Plates only work between devices on the same network, so nobody outside can address you.

  3. 3

    They accept, then it flows

    A first contact arrives as a request. Once accepted — and optionally saved — everything else from that plate arrives straight away.

Questions

Can anyone who guesses my plate send me things?

They have to be on your network, they only get five waiting items before being cut off, and their first contact sits as a request until you accept it. If someone is a nuisance, decline and block them, or issue yourself a new plate.

Does my plate ever change?

Only if you press New plate. It is stored on the server and survives restarts, and it is tied to this browser on this device — a different browser gets its own plate.

What does "the same network" mean exactly?

Self-hosted on a LAN, devices sharing a /24 subnet count as together. Behind a reverse proxy, devices arriving from the same public address count as together. Anyone else simply cannot see that your plate exists.

Where do my files go?

To the machine running this server, under a random filename, and they are deleted when the countdown ends, when either side deletes them, or when a request is declined. Nothing is sent to an external service.

Is it encrypted?

Traffic is plain HTTP unless you put it behind a reverse proxy with TLS. On a home or office network that is usually fine; on a shared network, terminate HTTPS in front of it.

What are the limits?

Files up to 512 MB each, 20 at a time, 80 items per device.
